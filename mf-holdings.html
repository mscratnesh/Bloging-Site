Rules, data source and limits

Data source

  • The monthly portfolio statements SEBI requires every fund house to publish by the 10th of the following month, downloaded from the fund houses' own websites (AMFI's portfolio disclosure page links to them).
  • Covered fund houses: SBI, ICICI Prudential, HDFC, Nippon India, Aditya Birla Sun Life, Axis, UTI, Mirae Asset, DSP, PPFAS and Motilal Oswal. Kotak isn't covered because its website blocks automated downloads. Other fund houses may be added later.
  • Updated on the 15th of each month for the month just ended. A fund house that hasn't published by then keeps its previous month until it does; each fund shows its own "as of" date.

How the numbers are worked out

  1. Weights are each holding's % of the fund's net assets, as the fund house reports it. Holdings with an ISIN are listed; cash, deposits, repo and receivables usually have none, so a fund's weights can add up to under 100%.
  2. Asset mix follows the sections of the statement: Indian equity, foreign equity, debt and money market, units of other funds, REITs/InvITs, and derivatives. Derivative positions, such as an arbitrage fund's short futures, can be negative.
  3. Sectors are the industry labels each fund house uses, which mostly follow AMFI's classification but can differ slightly between fund houses.
  4. Overlap between two funds = the sum, over stocks both hold, of the smaller of the two weights (equity only).
  5. Combined look-through = for each stock, the sum over your funds of (amount in the fund × the stock's weight). Everything that isn't equity is grouped as "Debt, cash & others".
  6. Stocks are matched across funds by ISIN, so the same company always lines up even if fund houses spell its name differently.

Limits

  • Portfolios are a month-end snapshot published up to about 45 days later; funds may have changed their holdings since.
  • Statements are read automatically and can contain errors. Check the fund house's own statement before relying on any figure.
  • Fund-of-funds show the funds they hold, not those funds' underlying stocks.
