How to read this

Beta measures how much a stock has moved, on average, for every 1% move in Nifty 50 over the last year. A beta of 1.3 means roughly 1.3% for a 1% Nifty move. Below 1 is calmer than the market; above 1 amplifies it.

Portfolio beta is the value-weighted average of your stocks' and funds' betas. Debt, gold and cash are treated as beta 0. Market-driven (R²) shows how much of a stock's daily movement Nifty explains. When it is low, the beta is less reliable and the stock follows its own story.

Beta is backward-looking. It changes over time and does not capture stock-specific risk, so treat it as a guide, not a guarantee.

Rules, data source and limits

Data source

  • Prices: NSE cash-market end-of-day bhavcopy files (UDiFF format) from NSE's public archives at nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/cm/. Only the betas worked out from them are used on this page.
  • Benchmark: NIFTYBEES (Nippon India ETF Nifty 50 BeES), a Nifty 50 ETF listed in the same file, used as the stand-in for Nifty 50. It tracks the index closely but not perfectly.
  • Window: the latest 250 trading days of daily returns (about one year), ending on the date shown under the paste box.
  • Mutual fund NAVs: AMFI's daily NAV history for every open-ended scheme (portal.amfiindia.com), Growth plans only.
  • Hedge prices: NIFTY futures and put closing prices, lot size and Nifty spot from the latest NSE F&O bhavcopy.
  • Updates: stock and fund betas once a week, on Friday evening after NSE publishes the day's file (fund NAVs may run to Thursday, as AMFI publishes late at night). Between updates the betas are up to a week old. Hedge prices update every trading day.

How each stock's beta is calculated

  1. Daily return = today's close ÷ previous close − 1, using NSE's own previous-close figure. NSE adjusts that figure on the ex-date of a split or bonus, so those don't show up as false crashes. Dividends are not adjusted for; their effect is small.
  2. Series counted: EQ, BE and BZ (normal and trade-for-trade segments). ETFs trade in EQ, so they are included.
  3. Bad prints removed: any daily return above +35% or below −35% is treated as a data error or relisting day and left out.
  4. Minimum history: a stock needs at least 120 days on which both it and NIFTYBEES have a return. Newer or rarely traded stocks get no beta and show as "Not found".
  5. Beta = covariance(stock, NIFTYBEES) ÷ variance(NIFTYBEES), over the shared days.
  6. Market-driven (R²) = the square of the correlation between the stock's and NIFTYBEES's daily returns.

How your portfolio's numbers are calculated

  1. Holding value: your Value column if you pasted one; otherwise Qty × your LTP/Price column; otherwise Qty × the last close in our data.
  2. Repeated symbols (for example, the same stock held in two accounts) are added together.
  3. Weight = holding value ÷ total equity value. Equity beta = sum of weight × beta over all matched stocks. A stock's Contribution is its weight × beta.
  4. Total portfolio beta (shown when you fill the non-equity box) = equity beta × equity value ÷ (equity value + non-equity value). Debt, FDs, gold and cash count as beta 0.
  5. If Nifty falls 10% = beta × 10%, and in rupees beta × value × 10%. It uses total portfolio beta and total value when you fill the non-equity box, and equity beta and equity value otherwise.
  6. Nifty hedge to neutralise = equity beta × equity value: the approximate notional of short Nifty futures that would offset the equity part's market moves.
  7. Labels: above 1.15 is Aggressive, 0.85 to 1.15 is Market-like, below 0.85 is Defensive. The same bands colour each stock's beta in the table.
  8. Warnings: symbols we can't match are listed, never silently dropped, and so are rows with no quantity or value. Any stock above 5% of your equity with R² below 15% is flagged as having a less reliable beta.

Mutual funds

  1. No portfolio holdings needed: a fund's beta comes from its own daily NAVs. Daily return = NAV ÷ the previous trading day's NAV − 1, read on the same trading days as NIFTYBEES, over the same 250 days. Beta and R² are then worked out exactly as for stocks, so stocks and funds add up in one portfolio beta.
  2. Growth plans only. IDCW (dividend) plans' NAVs drop on payout days, which would look like falls. Direct and Regular plans are listed separately; their betas are nearly the same.
  3. Bad prints removed: a daily NAV move above ±15% is treated as a data error. At least 120 shared days are needed.
  4. Matching: by ISIN or AMFI scheme code when you paste one (exact), otherwise by name: every word you paste must appear in the fund's name, on the same plan (Direct only if your text says Direct), and the closest name wins. Name matches are listed so you can check them; any marked "?" had a near tie.
  5. Overseas funds (fund-of-funds overseas, US and global funds) are flagged: their NAVs follow markets that close after India, so their betas against Nifty read too low.
  6. Debt and liquid funds come out near beta 0, which is correct. You can paste them or put their value in the non-equity box.

Hedge sizer

  1. Amount to hedge starts at your holdings beta × holdings value (the Nifty-equivalent exposure). Change it or pick a hedge percentage.
  2. Futures lots = amount ÷ (futures price × lot size), rounded to the nearest whole lot. Expiries are the monthly NIFTY futures at least 7 days away.
  3. Put lots = amount ÷ (Nifty spot × lot size), rounded. Cost = lots × lot size × the put's closing premium. The default strike is the one nearest 5% below spot.
  4. 10% fall example: portfolio fall ≈ amount × 10%. Futures gain ≈ lots × lot value × 10%. Puts pay (strike − 90% of spot) × lot size × lots at expiry, less the premium.
  5. Prices are the last NSE closing prices, not live quotes; a strike that traded fewer than 100 contracts is marked as thin. Margins, brokerage, taxes, rollover cost and basis are not included.

What you can paste

  • Rows copied from Excel or Google Sheets (tab-separated; Download sample (CSV) gives a template to fill in), CSV (quoted numbers such as "1,187.50" are fine), or columns separated by spaces.
  • Columns are recognised by header: Symbol, Instrument, Scrip, Stock, Ticker, Trading symbol, Security, Company, Name, Fund or Scheme for the holding; ISIN; Qty, Quantity, Shares or Units for quantity; Value, Cur. val, Market value, Present value or Holding value for value; LTP, Last, CMP, Close, Current price, Market price, Price or NAV for price. A Zerodha holdings export works as-is.
  • No header row? The columns are read as Symbol, Qty and an optional Price.
  • Symbols are cleaned: NSE:INFY, wipro.ns and HDFCBANK-BE become INFY, WIPRO and HDFCBANK. Use NSE symbols, not company names (HDFCBANK, not HDFC Bank Ltd). Rows starting with "Total" or "Grand" are skipped. ₹ signs and commas are ignored.
  • Not covered: bonds, SGBs, BSE-only stocks, closed-ended funds and IDCW plans. Add their value in the non-equity box instead.

Limits

  • Beta describes the past year. It changes over time and is not a forecast.
  • It captures only market-linked risk. Company news, sector shocks and liquidity are not in it.
  • The 10% fall and hedge figures are rough, linear estimates. Real moves in a sell-off can be larger.
  • Data may contain errors or gaps. Check anything important against another source before acting.

All parsing and calculation happens in your browser. Your holdings are never uploaded or stored. The page only downloads the shared beta table.

Questions

What is portfolio beta?

Portfolio beta is the value-weighted average of the betas of the stocks you hold. It shows how much your portfolio has tended to move for every 1% move in Nifty 50. A beta of 1.2 means about 1.2% for a 1% Nifty move; below 1 means calmer than the market.

How is each stock's beta calculated here?

From the last 250 trading days of NSE end-of-day bhavcopy data. Each day's return is the close divided by NSE's previous close minus 1, so splits and bonuses are already adjusted. Beta is the covariance of the stock's daily returns with NIFTYBEES (a Nifty 50 ETF) divided by the variance of NIFTYBEES's returns.

What is a good portfolio beta?

There is no single right number; it depends on your goals and how much fall you can sit through. This tool calls a beta above 1.15 aggressive, 0.85 to 1.15 market-like and below 0.85 defensive. Adding debt, gold or cash lowers your total portfolio beta.

Can I include mutual funds?

Yes. Paste the scheme name, ISIN or AMFI code with units or current value. A fund's beta comes from its own daily NAVs against NIFTYBEES over the same year as the stocks, so no holdings data is needed. Only open-ended Growth plans are covered, and overseas funds are flagged because their betas read too low.

How does portfolio beta relate to hedging with Nifty futures?

The Nifty notional that would offset the market part of your holdings is roughly beta times holdings value. For example, ₹10 lakh of stocks with a beta of 1.2 moves like about ₹12 lakh of Nifty. The hedge sizer turns that into Nifty futures or put lots at the last closing prices. Hedges cost money and don't remove stock-specific risk, so treat this as a learning figure, not advice.

Is my holdings data uploaded anywhere?

No. Your paste is read and calculated entirely in your browser. The page only downloads the shared table of betas; your holdings are never sent to a server or stored.

How often are the betas updated?

Once a week, on Friday evening after NSE publishes the day's bhavcopy. The date the betas run to is shown under the paste box.
